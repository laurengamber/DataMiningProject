Purpose
The purpose of this report is to produce a coherent working manuscript that is approaching conference paper quality. This is not the final project submission, and the analysis does not need to be fully complete. However, the report should read as a unified paper rather than a collection of notes or disconnected project updates.

This submission must revise and expand Progress Report 2. It should demonstrate that the central analytical pipeline is operational, most major experiments have been conducted, and the remaining work can reasonably be completed before the final deadline.

Required content
1. Title, abstract, and keywords
Provide an informative title, a revised abstract, and appropriate keywords. The abstract should accurately represent the current state of the work. Do not present planned analyses as completed findings.

2. Introduction
Present a polished introduction that establishes:

The problem and its significance

The limitation or gap addressed

The research questions or objectives

The approach taken

The principal contributions supported by the work completed so far

3. Related work
Provide a focused and well organized synthesis of relevant scholarship. The section should establish the intellectual context of the work and distinguish the project from prior studies.

4. Data
Provide a complete description of the dataset, sample construction, variables, preprocessing, quality concerns, and relevant ethical or privacy issues.

All important exclusions, transformations, and feature construction decisions should be documented.

5. Methodology
Present the baseline and primary methods with sufficient technical detail for evaluation and reproduction. Include equations, algorithm descriptions, model diagrams, or pseudocode when they improve clarity.

Explain why the chosen methods are appropriate and how they address the research questions.

6. Experimental design
Describe the complete evaluation protocol, including:

Training, validation, and testing procedures

Hyperparameter selection

Evaluation metrics

Comparison methods

Repeated trials or uncertainty estimates

Leakage prevention

Robustness, sensitivity, subgroup, or ablation analyses

Computational resources

7. Preliminary results
Present the results completed by the submission date. The section should contain well designed tables and figures rather than raw software output.

For each major result:

State the question being examined

Present the relevant evidence

Explain the observed pattern

Avoid claims that exceed the evidence

Identify whether the result is preliminary or final

Most core baseline and primary model results should be available by this stage. Secondary experiments, extended robustness checks, or refinements may remain incomplete.

8. Preliminary discussion
Interpret the current findings in relation to the research questions and prior literature. Discuss:

What has been learned

Why the observed results may have occurred

Practical or scientific implications

Important errors or failure cases

Alternative explanations

Limitations of the data and methodology

Appropriate boundaries on generalization

If important analyses remain incomplete, make the conditional nature of the interpretation explicit.

9. Responsible use and limitations
Address risks or limitations relevant to the project, such as:

Sampling or representation bias

Unequal performance across populations

Privacy or sensitive information

Potential misuse

Measurement and construct validity

Environmental or computational cost

Limitations created by artificial intelligence generated data, labels, code, or text

10. Preliminary conclusion
Summarize the current findings and their significance. The conclusion may be revised after the remaining analyses are completed.

11. Completion plan
Include a concise plan identifying exactly what will change between Progress Report 3 and the final submission. Use a table with the following columns:

Remaining task

Reason the task is necessary

Responsible group member

Expected output

Completion date

The plan should identify unfinished experiments, robustness checks, visualizations, writing, code validation, and manuscript editing.

12. Response to feedback
Explain how feedback from Progress Report 2 was addressed. Highlight major changes to the analysis or manuscript.

Submission requirements
Submit one PDF per group.

Use the approved conference template.

The manuscript should not exceed eight pages, excluding references.

Submit or link to the current code and documentation when permitted.

Tables and figures must be legible, numbered, captioned, and discussed in the text.

Clearly label incomplete experiments or preliminary findings.

Do not use placeholders such as “results will be added” without explaining the exact planned analysis in the completion plan.

Evaluation criteria
The report will be evaluated based on:

Quality of revisions made in response to prior feedback

Coherence and completeness of the working manuscript

Technical quality of the methodology

Credibility and reproducibility of the experimental design

Amount and quality of completed analysis

Clarity and validity of preliminary results

Depth and restraint of interpretation

Quality of figures and tables

Recognition of limitations and responsible use concerns

Feasibility of the final completion plan

Scholarly writing and adherence to conference conventions

This report is not expected to be the final paper. However, unresolved work should consist primarily of clearly defined experiments, validation, interpretation, and refinement rather than fundamental uncertainty about the project’s question, data, or methodology.
