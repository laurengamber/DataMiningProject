Purpose
The purpose of this report is to convert the paper blueprint into a partially completed conference manuscript and demonstrate meaningful technical progress. This is a cumulative submission. You must revise the material from Progress Report 1 based on feedback rather than submitting only the newly completed work.

By this stage, the research problem, data, methodology, and experimental design should be sufficiently stable to support implementation and analysis.

Required content
1. Revised title and abstract
Provide a revised working title. Include a preliminary abstract that concisely addresses:

The problem and motivation

The data

The methods being investigated

The evaluation approach

The anticipated or preliminary contribution

Because the analysis may not yet be complete, clearly label preliminary findings and avoid unsupported claims.

2. Introduction
Submit a developed introduction in complete prose. It should establish the problem, motivation, research gap, research questions, and expected contribution.

The final paragraph should summarize the paper’s specific contributions. These contributions may be revised as the project develops.

3. Related work
Submit a developed related work section that synthesizes and critically compares relevant research. Organize the literature around concepts, methods, findings, or limitations rather than summarizing one paper at a time.

The section should explain how the project differs from or builds upon prior work.

4. Data and preprocessing
Present a substantive description of the data and the completed or planned preprocessing pipeline. Include:

Source, access, and unit of analysis

Sample size, variables, labels, and class distribution

Data quality findings

Inclusion and exclusion decisions

Missing data treatment

Feature construction or representation

Measures taken to prevent leakage

Ethical, privacy, licensing, and representativeness concerns

Include informative descriptive statistics and visualizations where appropriate.

5. Methodology
Present the methodology in enough detail that another technically qualified person could understand and eventually reproduce the study. Include:

Baseline method

Primary methods

Mathematical formulation or algorithmic explanation when appropriate

Model configurations and important hyperparameters

Rationale for each method

Planned comparisons and ablations

Implementation environment

Distinguish clearly between methods already implemented and methods that remain planned.

6. Experimental design
Explain how the methods will be evaluated. Include:

Data splitting or resampling strategy

Performance metrics

Hyperparameter selection procedure

Comparison protocol

Random seeds or repeated trials when relevant

Statistical uncertainty or significance assessment when appropriate

Robustness, sensitivity, subgroup, or error analyses

Computational resources and constraints

The experimental design should match the intended use of the model. Random splitting should not be used when temporal, spatial, individual, or group dependence requires a different strategy.

7. Implementation progress and initial findings
Provide evidence of substantive technical progress. Depending on the project, this may include:

A completed preprocessing pipeline

Exploratory analysis

Baseline results

Initial model results

Preliminary patterns or discovered structures

Initial error analysis

Runtime or scalability findings

Implementation challenges and attempted solutions

Include at least one meaningful quantitative result, table, figure, or completed analytical output unless the instructor has approved an exception.

Clearly distinguish established findings from tentative observations.

8. Updated paper outline
Update the complete paper outline from Progress Report 1. For sections that are not yet written, state the intended content, required evidence, and anticipated tables or figures.

9. Response to feedback
Briefly explain how feedback on Progress Report 1 was addressed. Identify any major change to the research question, data, methods, evaluation plan, or scope.

10. Remaining work
Provide a specific plan for reaching Progress Report 3. Identify:

Experiments still to be completed

Technical problems that must be resolved

Additional literature or analysis required

Tables and figures still to be produced

Responsibilities of each group member

Any change to the project timeline or scope

Submission requirements
Submit one PDF per group.

Use the same approved conference format used for Progress Report 1.

The report should not exceed six pages, excluding references.

Submit or link to a reproducible code repository unless an exception is required for privacy or licensing reasons.

Number and caption every figure and table.

Cite all data, code, methods, and scholarly sources.

Clearly label incomplete sections and preliminary results.

Evaluation criteria
The report will be evaluated based on:

Quality of revisions made in response to feedback

Clarity and maturity of the introduction and research questions

Quality and synthesis of related work

Depth of data understanding and preprocessing

Technical justification and reproducibility of the methodology

Soundness of the experimental design

Evidence of meaningful implementation progress

Appropriate interpretation of initial findings

Feasibility and specificity of the remaining work plan

Overall organization and conference style presentation

Progress will be assessed relative to the project’s approved scope. Complex models alone do not demonstrate strong progress if the baseline, evaluation design, or data pipeline is not credible.
