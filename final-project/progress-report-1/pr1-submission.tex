\textbf{Working draft:}
News coverage of self-driving cars spans technological development, safety, regulation, and commercial deployment. The articles come from a diverse set of news sources, and these data remain largely unstructured and vast; as a result, the public may find it difficult to identify themes across various news sources. Identifying recurring themes and comparing their coverage requires analyzing a large collection of unstructured text. Topic modeling
offers a way to organize these articles into interpretable themes.

Our work supports three primary research questions: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item What themes characterize news coverage of autonomous vehicles from 2016 to 2020?
    \item How does the prevalence of these themes differ across publications?
    \item How does the method of choice affect themes identified? 
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Working draft:}
\subsection*{Overview}
We compare a classical Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) baseline with a modern BERTopic pipeline. Both pipelines are applied to the same set of 7,337 articles from 25 publications (January 2016 to March 2020), using a shared duplicate-handling policy and preprocessing suited to each representation.

\subsection*{Classical Pipeline (LDA)}
The classical pipeline follows four stages \parencite{qamar2024}: (1) data preprocessing, (2) document-term representation, (3) topic model fitting, and (4) topic interpretation. We tokenize article bodies, remove English stopwords, and build a document-term count matrix. LDA provides an interpretable baseline through its topic-word distributions and article-level topic mixtures. We compare a small set of topic counts and assign each article its highest-weight (dominant) topic for publication summaries, while retaining the full topic mixture.

\subsection*{Modern Pipeline (BERTopic)}
The modern pipeline follows five stages \parencite{alammar2024, kumari2026}: (1) data preprocessing, (2) document embedding, (3) dimensionality reduction, (4) document clustering, and (5) topic representation and interpretation. We embed articles with GTE-small to capture contextual similarity, reduce dimensionality with UMAP, cluster with HDBSCAN, and extract topic keywords with class-based TF-IDF. Embedding input retains sentence context after basic cleaning, and long articles are embedded in chunks that are combined into a single article representation. Because HDBSCAN allows articles to remain unassigned, we report the unassigned proportion explicitly. We examine a limited set of UMAP neighborhood sizes and HDBSCAN minimum cluster sizes.

\subsection*{Topic Labeling and Publication Comparison}
For each pipeline, we label topics using top keywords and representative articles, then produce heat maps of topic proportions within each publication. These comparisons are reported alongside publication sample sizes and the modern model's unassigned proportion. Differences are interpreted descriptively, taking into account unequal publication and date coverage.
