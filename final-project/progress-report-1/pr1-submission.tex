\textbf{Working draft:}
News coverage of self-driving cars spans technological development, safety, regulation, and commercial deployment. The articles come from a diverse set of news sources, and these data remain largely unstructured and vast; as a result, the public may find it difficult to identify themes across various news sources. Identifying recurring themes and comparing their coverage requires analyzing a large collection of unstructured text. Topic modeling
offers a way to organize these articles into interpretable themes.

Our work supports three primary research questions: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item What themes characterize news coverage of autonomous vehicles from 2016 to 2020?
    \item How does the prevalence of these themes differ across publications?
    \item How does the method of choice affect themes identified? 
\end{itemize}

\section*{Methodology}

\textbf{Purpose:} Describe how the topic modeling analysis of self-driving car news coverage is being performed.

\textbf{Planned content:}

    \item Corpus construction, data audit, and duplicate handling \done
    \item Classical pipeline: LDA baseline \wip
    \item Modern pipeline: BERTopic \wip
    \item Topic labeling and publication comparisons \planned
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Working draft:}
\subsection*{Overview}
We compare a classical Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) baseline with a modern BERTopic pipeline. Both pipelines are applied to the same set of 7,337 articles from 25 publications (January 2016 to March 2020), using a shared duplicate-handling policy and preprocessing suited to each representation.

\subsection*{Classical Pipeline (LDA)}
The classical pipeline follows four stages \parencite{qamar2024}: (1) data preprocessing, (2) document-term representation, (3) topic model fitting, and (4) topic interpretation. We tokenize article bodies, remove English stopwords, and build a document-term count matrix. LDA provides an interpretable baseline through its topic-word distributions and article-level topic mixtures. We compare a small set of topic counts and assign each article its highest-weight (dominant) topic for publication summaries, while retaining the full topic mixture.

\subsection*{Modern Pipeline (BERTopic)}
The modern pipeline follows five stages \parencite{alammar2024, kumari2026}: (1) data preprocessing, (2) document embedding, (3) dimensionality reduction, (4) document clustering, and (5) topic representation and interpretation. We embed articles with GTE-small to capture contextual similarity, reduce dimensionality with UMAP, cluster with HDBSCAN, and extract topic keywords with class-based TF-IDF. Embedding input retains sentence context after basic cleaning, and long articles are embedded in chunks that are combined into a single article representation. Because HDBSCAN allows articles to remain unassigned, we report the unassigned proportion explicitly. We examine a limited set of UMAP neighborhood sizes and HDBSCAN minimum cluster sizes.

\subsection*{Topic Labeling and Publication Comparison}
For each pipeline, we label topics using top keywords and representative articles, then produce heat maps of topic proportions within each publication. These comparisons are reported alongside publication sample sizes and the modern model's unassigned proportion. Differences are interpreted descriptively, taking into account unequal publication and date coverage.

\textbf{Working draft:}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Define the shared corpus and experimental settings.} We will use the same article set and duplicate-handling policy for both pipelines. Article bodies will be teh modeling input, while publication and date will remain metadata. We will document preprocessing choices, hyperparameters, and random seeds.
    \item \textbf{Develop the LDA baseline.} We will construct a document-term count matrix and compare a small set of topic counts. We will retain article-level topic mixtures and use each article's highest-weight topic for publication summaries. 
    \item \textbf{Develop the BERTopic pipeline.} We will generate GTE-small article embeddings, reduce dimensionality with UMAP, and cluster articles with HDBSCAN. We will compare a limited set of neighborhood sizes and minimum cluser sizes, recording topic counts, sizes and the proportion of unassigned articles.
    \item \textbf{Select configurations and label topics}.We will consider coherence, topic sizes, keywords, and representative articles when selecting one configuration per pipeline. Configurations and labels will be finalized before independent human evaluation. Final evaluation articles and duplicate copies will be excluded from development review and topic naming.
    \item \textbf{Independent human evaluation.} Two team members will independently review the same 100 randomly selected articles using a fixed seed. They will rate how well each assigned topic describes the article on a 1–5 scale. We will summarize ratings and reviewer agreement, recording ambiguous and unassigned cases separately.
    \item \textbf{Compare topics across publications. } or each pipeline, we will calculate the proportion of each publication’s articles assigned to each topic. Heat maps and representative articles will help identify shared themes and differences in thematic emphasis. We will report publication sample sizes, include BERTopic’s unassigned category, and consider unequal date coverage when interpreting differences.
    \item \textbf{Compare pipeline outputs.} We will compare coherence, keyword diversity, human ratings, computational cost, and topic overlap. We will also examine whether both methods support similar conclusions about publication differences, accounting for differences in topic granularity.
    \item \textbf{Assess robustness.} We will repeat selected configurations with different random seeds and examine sensitivity hyperparameter selection. Where article counts permit, we will restrict publication comparisons to shared periods to assess the influence of unequal date coverage. We will assess whether the main themes and publication patterns persist.
    \item \textbf{Evaluate success and document conclusions}. Success will mean identifying article-supported themes, characterizing their prevalence across publications, and explaining how these findings depend on the modeling method. Findings and conclusions will be expanded once experimentation and analysis are complete.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Limitations and Responsible Use Considerations}
\textbf{Purpose:} Acknowledge issues and limitations of our corpus, methods, and evaluation.

\textbf{Planned content:} 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Uneven publication and date coverage \planned
    \item Imperfect filtering and repeated stories \planned
    \item Influence of major news events \planned
    \item Model assumptions and sensitivity \planned
    \item Limitations of Human evaluation \planned
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Working draft:}
The corpus may not represent all AV news coverage. Keyword filtering may omit relevant articles or retain irrelevant ones, while duplicate or syndicated stories may overrepresent particular themes. Unequal publication sizes and date coverage limit comparisons, especially for publications with few articles. Major news events may also produce temporary concentrations of coverage that do not reflect sustained thematic emphasis.

Both pipelines depend on preprocessing and modeling choices. LDA’s dominant-topic summaries simplify articles containing multiple themes, while BERTopic’s embeddings, chunk pooling, and clustering settings may affect topic boundaries and leave articles unassigned. Robustness checks will assess these effects. Human evaluation by two team members provides a limited, subjective assessment.
